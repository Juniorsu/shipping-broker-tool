Shipping Learning Desk

A practical voyage map for breakbulk brokers: from fixture recap and loading documents to freight, original bills, discharge and close-out.

Operational guide, not legal or P&I advice. Follow the signed charterparty, sale contract, bill wording, owner/agent instructions and local law. Discharging cargo from the vessel is different from releasing it to a receiver.

Voyage workflow · fixture to close-out

FIXTURE➜LOAD PORT➜B/L + FREIGHT➜SEA PASSAGE➜DISCHARGE➜CLOSE-OUT
1
Fix and confirm the voyage
Prepare
  • Fixture recap / charterparty and cargo nomination.
  • Load/discharge ports, quantity, laycan, freight and demurrage terms.
  • Document instructions, bill type and authorized issuer.
  • Sale Incoterm with precise named place/port and version.
Do
  • Check options, subjects, dates and who pays each cost.
  • Align sale-contract handoff with the carriage instructions.
  • Confirm agents, survey/tally needs and reporting contacts.
2
Plan before loading
Prepare
  • Pre-stowage plan (requesting it about a week ahead is a planning target).
  • Cargo particulars, stowage factor, dimensions and handling needs.
  • Port limits, draft, vessel gear, holds and terminal sequence.
Do
  • Review the proposed hold allocation and operational constraints with vessel/agent.
  • Resolve cargo compatibility, lifting, lashing or weather-protection questions.
  • Treat this as provisional planning; it is not the final stowage record.
3
Arrive at load port and load
Prepare
  • Arrival/NOR instructions and agent contact details.
  • Loading sequence, tally/survey instructions and cargo paperwork.
  • SOF log ready to capture every key event and interruption.
Do
  • Master/agent tenders NOR under the charterparty requirements.
  • Record start, stop, restart and completion times in the SOF.
  • Compare loaded quantity and apparent condition with the mate’s receipt; record exceptions accurately.
4
Reconcile cargo documents
Prepare
  • Mate’s receipt, cargo manifest and shipper’s B/L instructions.
  • Draft B/L details: vessel/voyage, ports, marks, quantity, dates and freight clause.
  • Final stowage plan based on the completed load.
Do
  • Cross-check the draft B/L against the mate’s receipt and verified loading figures.
  • Confirm on-board/received-for-shipment status and consignee/order wording.
  • Carry material condition remarks through accurately; do not create a false clean bill.
5
Settle freight and issue the B/L
Prepare
  • Owner/operator freight invoice and payment instructions.
  • Agreed wording: Freight Prepaid, Freight Payable at, or Collect.
  • Correct number of originals and authorized signature details.
Do
  • Reconcile the freight clause to the fixture and actual payment/release authorization.
  • Carrier/authorized agent signs and releases the stated original set.
  • Log original count, date, recipient and courier/document reference.
6
Track the sea passage and documents
Prepare
  • ETA updates, voyage contacts and any required arrival/manifest documents.
  • Original-document tracking details and named document recipients.
Do
  • Report schedule changes and keep the receiver/agent updated as agreed.
  • Track originals through shipper, bank and buyer where applicable.
  • Escalate missing or inconsistent originals early; a copy or invoice may not replace a required original.
7
Discharge and deliver cargo
Prepare
  • Discharge NOR requirements, receiving agent details and discharge plan.
  • Delivery document, endorsement and consignee-verification requirements.
  • Customs and terminal release requirements.
Do
  • Tender NOR as required; maintain SOF through discharge and record outturn/tally.
  • Separate unloading from cargo release: verify the controlling document, receiver and authority.
  • Record original surrender/cancellation where required; do not promise release on an unapproved LOI.
8
Close out the voyage
Prepare
  • Discharge SOF, outturn/tally/survey reports and agent statements.
  • Freight, port-cost and laytime records; open claims or exceptions.
Do
  • Reconcile quantity, time, freight, port costs and claims against the contract.
  • Settle accounts, preserve approvals/document versions and close outstanding cargo issues.

Document names and timing vary by charterparty, trade and port. The pre-stowage plan is provisional planning material; the final stowage plan and actual figures should reflect the completed load.

OBL essentials · common forms

OBL means an issued original bill of lading, not one special bill type. A B/L is a cargo receipt and evidence of the carriage contract; an order bill may also function as a transferable document of title depending on wording and law.

Order B/L
Common in breakbulk and documentary sales. Made “to order”; check endorsement chain and presentation requirements before delivery.
Straight B/L
Names the consignee. Not transferable by endorsement, but an original may still be required; check wording and applicable law.
Sea waybill
Common alternative for trusted parties; non-negotiable and usually no original surrender. It is not an OBL—follow its named-consignee and local delivery rules.
Original set
Check the number issued (for example, 3/3), consignee/order wording and surrender clause. Never assume one original cancels all others unless the bill says so.

On-board/received for shipment and clean/claused are status/condition notations, not B/L forms. A clean bill must not conceal known adverse remarks.

Freight wording on the B/L

Freight Prepaid
States freight is prepaid under the applicable arrangement. Use only as authorized and consistent with the fixture and payment status; it is not a broker’s assumption.
Freight Payable at
Names where freight is due under the bill/contract. Check the named place, payer and payment trigger exactly.
Freight Collect
Common wording for freight due from the receiver at destination, subject to the actual contract and bill terms.

These clauses describe freight arrangements; they do not by themselves decide title to cargo or who is entitled to delivery. Reconcile B/L wording with the signed fixture and owner’s release instructions.

Incoterms® · sale-contract handoff

Incoterms® rules allocate seller/buyer tasks, costs and risk transfer for delivery. They do not by themselves determine title, payment timing or the carrier’s cargo-release obligations. Always state the rule, precise named place/port and version (for example, FOB [named port] Incoterms® 2020).

Sea/inland-waterway
FAS (alongside ship), FOB (on board), CFR (cost and freight), CIF (cost, insurance and freight). For bulk/general cargo loaded directly aboard, FOB may fit the intended delivery point; choose based on the sale contract and ICC guidance.
Any transport mode
EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP. For containerized/multimodal cargo, compare FCA/CPT/CIP with sea-only FOB/CFR/CIF; select the rule that matches the actual delivery handoff.

Broker check: sale Incoterm and charterparty freight terms are separate contracts. Confirm who books/pays carriage and insurance, where risk transfers, and whether the named port/terminal and B/L instructions align.

LOIs · common requests and safeguards

A letter of indemnity (LOI) is a separate undertaking for a specified operation. It is not proof of title, an original B/L, or guaranteed insurance. The LOIs below are request categories, not one universal standard form.

Switching B/L LOI
For a requested replacement B/L set. Owner/carrier approval is essential; control and cancel the full first set, verify the legitimate reason, and keep parties, cargo, dates and routing accurate. Never facilitate concealment or fraud.
Clean B/L LOI
May be requested when shipper disputes a mate’s-receipt remark. Do not use an LOI to erase a known adverse condition or issue a knowingly inaccurate clean bill. Record actual condition and escalate for owner/club/legal review.
Telex release LOI
Terminology varies. A telex release usually follows issue and surrender of the full original set at origin, with carrier instruction to release at destination. It is not automatically an LOI or a substitute for surrender; confirm exactly what was issued, surrendered and approved.
Situational operations LOI
Examples include loading/discharging moisture-sensitive cargo in light rain, or another specifically requested operational deviation. Define the activity, cargo, limits and parties; owner must approve. An LOI never overrides safe-work rules, port authority directions or the charterparty.
  1. Write down the request, reason, operation, cargo/B/L references, parties and proposed timing.
  2. Check the charterparty and escalate to owner/operator. Obtain current owner/club or legal wording for that exact risk.
  3. Verify issuer identity, signatory authority, financial standing/security and every named detail.
  4. Wait for explicit written owner acceptance before issuing bills, changing instructions, or conducting/releasing cargo.
  5. Keep the signed LOI and approval chain, document the operation, and follow the agreed close-out/original-surrender steps.

Stop and escalate

  • Request to backdate or make a knowingly false/clean B/L despite a material remark.
  • Unclear original set, inconsistent endorsements, missing originals or disputed cargo ownership.
  • Unverified recipient or LOI signer, or pressure to act before written owner approval.
  • Switch request before the first set is controlled and cancelled, or a telex-release request without confirmed surrender status.
  • Unsafe weather/cargo operation, including pressure to work in rain outside agreed limits.

Knowingly inaccurate bills or delivery instructions can create serious liability and jeopardize P&I cover. Stop and refer the case to owner/operator and their P&I/legal contact.
